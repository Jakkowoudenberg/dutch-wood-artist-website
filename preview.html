Manifesto

Every person carries a story.

Like the rings of a tree, our experiences shape who we become. Every choice changes our path. Every encounter leaves a mark. Some people step into the light, while others remain unseen. Along the way we discover that true growth is not measured by what we achieve, but by what we pass on to others.

My work is not a collection of separate artworks. It is one continuous story about what it means to be human. From Tree Rings, to The Night Watch and Show Yourself, to United Wood Floor Layers – Pay It Forward, Between Doors, and Gemikigai — each project is a new chapter in the same journey.

I don’t create art to provide answers. I create art to ask questions. Questions about identity. About visibility. About courage. About connection. About legacy. About purpose.

Wood is my medium. The human journey is my subject.

Why

Every art floor has a reason why it exists.

I don't make floors to fill a space. Each work starts from an idea. Something about the place, the people who'll stand on it, the story a building wants to tell. It means something, and it keeps meaning long after it's laid. It's why no two are alike, and why a space that holds one is never quite the same again. It gives a space a soul.

The Night Watch in Wood

195,000 pixels · 52 wood species · 4 years

Created by Jakko Woudenberg, The Night Watch in Wood is a monumental contemporary interpretation of Rembrandt's iconic masterpiece. Every pixel individually selected and placed by hand, cut diagonally from face grain wood to reveal the grain at its most expressive.

Measuring 535 × 425 × 18 cm, the artwork occupies more than 20 square metres. Its modular construction allows both horizontal and vertical exhibition. Presented horizontally, it becomes a stage — an artwork that can be performed on, spoken from, and stood upon.

Unlike the original in the Rijksmuseum, which was cut down on all four sides in 1715 to fit its new location in Amsterdam's Town Hall, The Night Watch in Wood shows the complete composition as Rembrandt intended it.

More than a tribute, it tells two stories at once — Rembrandt's masterpiece and Jakko's perspective on it. About connection, craftsmanship, creativity, adventure, and the universal human desire to be seen. From a distance, you recognise the masterpiece. Up close, you discover the craftsmanship behind it.

Exhibited at Station Amstel, Amsterdam · WorldSkills the Finals, NL · NWFA Convention, Orlando, USA

The Artist

Artist Statement

I don’t make floors. I make monuments in wood — each built for a single place, and never made again. Every piece is cut and placed by hand, over months and sometimes years. It is not made to decorate a room, but to outlast it.

Artist Bio

Jakko Woudenberg is a Dutch master parquet craftsman and monumental artist, based in Schagen, the Netherlands, and a member of the Dutch Guild of Parquet Layers.

For decades he practised parquet as a trade. Since 2019 he has been on a mission to pull it into the art world — not because it sounds good, but because he believes an art form lies hidden inside the craft. INSSAEI and The Night Watch in Wood are his proof: the latter a monumental reconstruction of Rembrandt’s masterpiece built from 195,000 hand-cut wooden pixels across 52 species of wood, including the sections cut away from the original in 1715.

His work has been exhibited internationally, from Amsterdam to Orlando, and recognised with the Vakwerk Award, the WFB Design Award and the CFJ Award. He works only on commission, each piece conceived for a specific site and purpose.

He believes craft dies when it is kept to yourself. Through United Wood Floor Layers — Pay It Forward, he shares his knowledge freely: with the next generation, with colleagues, with anyone willing to learn.

The Movement

United Wood Floor Layers

A worldwide movement Jakko founded to connect craftspeople across borders. Every participant creates a panel and commits to helping three others. No ego, no money. Just skill, passed forward. Because the best things in craft were never built alone.
